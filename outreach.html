Causes I care about

Astronomy in Chile

Dark and Quiet Skies

Northern Chile has some of the darkest skies on Earth and hosts many of the world's major observatories, including Rubin. I support Chilean efforts to protect them from the light pollution of growing cities.

Science funding

Public Investment in Science

Science needs sustained, well-informed public support. At KIPAC's Visualization Lab, which turns large simulations into immersive 3D visualizations, I host policymakers, donors, funding agencies, and members of the public. I teach others how to bring in funding for fundamental science, and I show how investment in fundamental research leads to discovery, how its discoveries directly impact the general public, and how science can serve as a sandbox for the responsible use of AI/ML.

Conservation

Wetland and Native Habitat Restoration

Point Reyes has always been a special place to me. I volunteer on wetland and native-plant restoration there and around San Francisco Bay to help preserve its natural beauty, so it remains a place of leisure and inspiration.

How you can help

For scientists

KIPAC-Chile Engagement Program

I co-founded this program, which is funded by a KIPAC Innovation Grant. KIPAC members traveling to Chile can add a one- or two-day stop to visit a Chilean university, give a talk, and meet students and faculty. Travel support is available; sign up through the interest form on the KIPAC intranet.

Interest form Program website

Rubin 2036 Workshop

The first in a series of workshops shaping Rubin's science and instrumentation after the ten-year LSST survey. Researchers with ideas for the post-LSST era are encouraged to join.

Workshop page

La Serena Rubin Science Workshop

A meeting on early Rubin science held near the observatory, connecting the project with students and faculty at Chilean institutions.

For everyone

Chicken Ranch Beach Restoration

Volunteer days on Tomales Bay in Inverness, run by SPAWN and the Tomales Bay Foundation: planting, weeding, and caring for native wetland plants. No experience needed.

Volunteer days

Save The Bay

Hands-on marsh restoration and advocacy around San Francisco Bay, with regular volunteer events for individuals and groups.

Volunteer savesfbay.org

iNaturalist

Photograph the plants and animals you come across and share them on iNaturalist. Once the community confirms an identification, the observation becomes research-grade data that scientists and land managers use to track biodiversity, spot invasive species, and guide restoration work. It works anywhere, including on the mountains around Rubin: visitors to Cerro Tololo and Cerro Pachón can add their sightings to the observatories' biodiversity project.

Buy Local Honey

Honey is delicious, and bees make good friends. Buying from local beekeepers keeps pollinators healthy and supports the people who look after them. Whether I'm near home or traveling somewhere new, I always bring back a jar of the local honey.
