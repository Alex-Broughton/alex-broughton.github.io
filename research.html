Instrument signature removal for LSST

I lead ISR development, commissioning, and science validation for the Rubin Data Management Science Pipelines. The work covers the full chain of detector effects in thick, fully-depleted CCDs — bias structure and persistence, dark current, crosstalk, nonlinearity, charge transfer inefficiency, stray light, and above all the brighter-fatter effect, where charge already collected in a pixel deflects later arrivals and produces a flux-dependent PSF. I developed a physically-motivated correction that accounts for the higher-order, anisotropic evolution of the effect that the standard kernel method assumes away, and used the residuals to define a new full-well metric for LSSTCam sensors.

Calibration products and verification

Corrections are only as good as the calibration products behind them. I build and validate those products for LSSTCam, define the verification metrics that gate their release, and contributed to the ComCam on-sky calibration plan as part of the TAXICAB team. Earlier, I worked on LSSTCam electro-optical testing and focal-plane characterization at SLAC.

Weak lensing, shear, and cluster cosmology

In DESC's Pixel-to-Object working group I contribute forward models that propagate image systematics into object-level measurements — the step where a detector artifact becomes a bias in shear. Alongside that I work on shear mapping with metadetection catalogs, PSF modelling and rho-statistic diagnostics, star–galaxy separation, photometric redshifts, and galaxy cluster cosmology on Rubin data previews.

Machine learning for imaging and emission models

I use learned models where the physics is well-posed but the mapping is intractable: multi-method classification of stars and galaxies in Rubin DP2, classifiers for stray-light contamination in survey visits, and — in my thesis work — deep learning models of the discrete component of Galactic interstellar γ-ray emission, which sharpened the systematic error budget on the Fermi-LAT Galactic Center excess.
